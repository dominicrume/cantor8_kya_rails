## 1. Can Federico champion me?

**No. And I told you the wrong thing last time.**

I said "technically yes now, since there is no approved list any more." I had
read the workflow and not the rule. The rule is **CIP-0100**, and it is exact:

> "The Tech & Ops committee should request **one of its members** to become the
> 'champion' of the external contributor and work with them through the process
> and **represent them in the committee**."

A champion is not a supporter. It is **a seat**. It is a person who sits on the
Canton Foundation Tech & Ops committee and speaks for your proposal in a room
you are not in.

The structure:

- Any **Canton Foundation member organisation** may attend Tech & Ops meetings.
- The committee elects **five voting members and two alternates**, quarterly.
- Those five are who actually decide.

Moonsong Labs is a listed **ecosystem service provider**. That is a different
thing from a Foundation member organisation with a seat. Unless Moonsong holds
one, Federico cannot champion you no matter how much he likes the work.

**What he can do, which is worth more anyway:** introduce you to someone who
holds a seat, or say publicly that the design is sound. A Community Tech
Partner vouching for you to a Foundation member is the shortest real path.

**So the route is the SIG, and it is the route the process itself names.** Your
proposal is already labelled `regulatory-compliance`. Three of that SIG's four
members are at **Digital Asset**, which does hold a seat. The review document
says SIG members may self-assign proposals, and that SIG participation is not
limited to Foundation members.

You are not sneaking in. You are knocking where the sign says knock.

## 2. The line about Federico

**Post the fact. Never the implication.**

Say this, which anyone can verify in the thread:

> A Canton Community Tech Partner reviewed my design on the forum, found the
> flaw in it, and I shipped his fix the next day and credited him by name.

Do not say this, which he can correct in public:

> A Canton Community Tech Partner endorsed my project.

The first is a description of something that happened. The second is a claim
about what he thinks, and you do not know what he thinks.

**Why the first one is stronger anyway.** It shows you took criticism from an
expert and acted on it in a day. Anyone reading draws the conclusion themselves,
and a conclusion a reader reaches is held harder than one they were handed.

**Where to post it:** LinkedIn, with the forum link. Not a new forum thread.
Do not tag him. If he wants to be part of it he will comment, and that is worth
ten times a tag.

## 3. Contract Daml work, which is the money

**£675** is the median day rate for DAML contract roles in Central London,
against **£438** across all developers. Twenty days is £13,500.

**The path is not job boards.** There are almost no advertised Daml contracts,
and by the time one is advertised it has a hundred applicants. The path is the
firms that sell Canton engineering and subcontract when a client lands.

The Canton ecosystem directory lists them. The ones that sell engineering:

**The ask is the same one every time, and it is one question:** do you ever
subcontract Daml work when a client lands?

That is a normal professional question. It is not a favour and it is not a job
application. A service firm with more work than people says yes; one without
says no; neither costs you anything.

**What you send with it is shipped work, never ambition:**

- three merged pull requests into the Canton Developer Hub
- eight fixes to the Cantor8 toolkit, four issues and four pull requests
- a Daml package with 111 test scripts where deleting any spending rule turns a
  named test red
- a filed Dev Fund proposal, open, under RFP 27

**Send it to five of them this week, not one.** One reply out of five is a
normal rate and five asks is one afternoon. Sending to one and waiting is how a
month goes by.
